# RightWalk Foundation: Economic Impact Summary

### Conservative Estimate -- Wage-Only Benefits

---

## Headline Results

| Intervention | BCR (RWF Investment) | BCR (Total Investment) | Lifetime Benefit per Beneficiary |
|:-------------|:---------------------|:-----------------------|:--------------------------------|
| **RTE (Private School Access)** | **~82:1** | **~3.2:1** | ~Rs 3.3 Lakhs NPV |
| **Apprenticeship (NATS)** | **~133:1** | **~5.1:1** | ~Rs 8.0 Lakhs NPV |

*Moderate scenario, West India, male, urban reference. Values are Net Present Value of lifetime earnings differential, discounted at 5% over 40-year career.*

---

## What Was Measured

We estimated the **lifetime earnings difference** between program beneficiaries and a counterfactual group who did not participate, using India-specific labor market data (PLFS 2023-24) and published academic research. Benefits are measured **only in wage terms** -- the most conservative and defensible metric.

## Key Results

| Metric | RTE | Apprenticeship |
|:-------|:----|:---------------|
| RWF cost per beneficiary | Rs 4,000 | Rs 6,000 |
| Total cost (incl. govt/employer) | Rs 1,04,000 | Rs 1,58,460 |
| Formal sector entry (treatment) | 30% | 68% |
| Formal sector entry (counterfactual) | 9.1% | 10% |
| Primary value driver | Formal employment pathway (79%) | Formal placement + skill premium |

## What This INCLUDES

- Lifetime earnings differentials (formal vs. informal sector)
- Experience-based wage growth (2.7%/yr formal, 1.2%/yr informal)
- Education quality premium (Mincer returns, RTE only)
- Opportunity costs (apprenticeship training year)
- Discounting at 5% social discount rate

## What This Does NOT Include (Conservative by Design)

- Tax revenue to government (est. +10-15%)
- Health benefits from ESI/PF access
- Intergenerational effects on beneficiaries' children
- Reduced welfare dependency
- Social mobility and civic participation
- Non-economic outcomes (self-esteem, aspirations)

**We deliberately exclude these to avoid overstating impact. The BCR figures represent the floor, not the ceiling, of program value.**

## Top 5 Assumptions

| # | Assumption | Value | Source |
|:--|:-----------|:------|:-------|
| 1 | RTE graduates enter formal sector at 3.3x baseline | 30% (vs. 9.1%) | RWF expert guidance; **no empirical data** |
| 2 | Apprentice formal placement rate | 68% | RWF operational tracking data |
| 3 | Returns to schooling | 5.8%/year | Chen et al. (2022), PLFS 2018-19 |
| 4 | Apprentice skill premium half-life | 10 years | International proxy (no India data) |
| 5 | Social discount rate | 5% | Murty & Panda (2020), Ramsey formula |

## Confidence Level

**Moderate overall** -- but with a critical caveat: the #1 driver of RTE value (formal sector entry rate) has zero empirical grounding. One parameter (P_FORMAL_RTE) accounts for 79% of RTE benefits, and it is based entirely on expert judgment.

## Next Steps

A **tracer study** (Rs 45-55 lakhs, 18 months) would validate or revise the key assumptions with RWF-specific data. This single investment closes 7 of 8 evidence gaps identified in the model audit.
